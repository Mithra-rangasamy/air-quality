ℹ️ What Does This Mean? (Everyday Language Guide)

🟢 🟡 🟠 🔴 Air Quality Index (AQI) Color Bands

AQI translates complex chemical measurements into an easy-to-understand scale:

0 - 50 Good (Green): Air is clean, fresh, and poses no risk to anyone.
51 - 100 Satisfactory (Yellow): Minor breathing discomfort to unusually sensitive individuals.
101 - 200 Moderate (Orange): Causes breathing difficulty for children, elderly, and asthma patients.
201 - 300+ Hazardous (Red): Serious health danger for the entire population. Use N95 masks outdoors.

🔬 What are PM2.5 and PM10?

PM2.5: Microscopic particles under 2.5 microns (30x thinner than a hair) from combustion and industrial emissions. Because they are tiny, they bypass your throat filters, penetrate deep into the lungs, and directly enter your bloodstream.

PM10: Coarse dust and road soot under 10 microns that irritate the eyes, nasal passages, and upper throat.

📊 How Does the Health Risk Score Work?

Unlike traditional dashboards that only look at sensor numbers, our Big Data system merges 70% ambient sensor readings with 30% citizen symptom reports.

When citizens report coughing, breathlessness, or eye burning, the risk score escalates immediately, triggering health advisories even before sensors register peak smog.

🚨 What is a Compound Health Convergence Alert?

A high-priority alert triggered when severe air pollution coincides directly with an anomalous spike in citizen symptom reports. This flags confirmed local health outbreaks where pollution is actively harming residents right now.
